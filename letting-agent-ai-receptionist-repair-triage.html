AI Repair Call Triage for UK Letting Agents

A practical intake, escalation and handover checklist for tenant maintenance enquiries. Built for agency review, not automated safety decisions.

Published 31 October 2026 · Educational workflow

The problem to solve

A repair report can arrive while staff are at viewings, after hours or through an inbox that nobody is monitoring. An AI receptionist can help capture consistent details and route the report, but it must not decide whether a property is safe or promise a contractor has been dispatched. This is a sample operating design, not a completed Zovariq client deployment.

Minimum intake fields

Ask for the property reference or address, a callback method, a short description of the issue, when it started and whether the caller believes there is immediate danger. Avoid collecting identity documents, unnecessary medical details or a long free-text history. Offer a human route immediately; do not make the tenant finish the form to access emergency guidance.

Four routing scenarios

  1. Routine issue: record the report and send to the designated property manager; acknowledge delivery only when the destination confirms receipt.
  2. Unclear severity: record uncertainty and alert a human; never label a problem safe.
  3. Potential emergency: stop normal questioning and show the agency-approved emergency contact instructions; for immediate danger direct the caller to emergency services.
  4. Failed integration: state that the report was not confirmed delivered and provide a verified fallback contact route.

Example conversation: reported leak

Tenant: There is water coming through the ceiling.

Assistant: I am an automated assistant. If anyone is in immediate danger, use the appropriate emergency services. I can show your agency's emergency contact now. Would you like that contact, or would you like me to record a report for staff? I cannot assess whether the area is safe.

Note: The agency must approve actual safety wording, escalation routes and out-of-hours arrangements before launch.

Five pilot acceptance tests

  1. Submit a routine repair during office hours and confirm the correct inbox receives it.
  2. Submit the same report after hours and check the approved fallback.
  3. Simulate a potentially dangerous leak and check that human escalation appears immediately.
  4. Disconnect the destination inbox and confirm the assistant does not claim delivery.
  5. Request deletion or access to a report and verify the response matches the actual privacy procedure.

Privacy and governance

Call transcripts, addresses and contact details may be personal data. Document the lawful basis, purpose, processors, access permissions and retention schedule before deploying. For UK organisations, consult the ICO's data protection guidance. This page is not legal or emergency advice.

Pilot measurement

Track verified report delivery, staff corrections, time to human acknowledgement, incomplete intake rates and failed handovers. Compare against a baseline. Do not infer saved revenue, resolved repairs or legal compliance from chatbot message counts.
