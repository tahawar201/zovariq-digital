FOR B2B MARKETING TEAMS

What to include in a content repurposing project brief

Before choosing a webinar or podcast repurposing service, define the job the content needs to do. A useful brief reduces revision cycles and makes it easier to judge the finished work.

A

Audience and purpose

Describe the people you want to reach, the questions they ask, the offer or resource each asset should point to, and the channels that actually matter. A product explainer for prospects needs a different edit from a customer-training recap.

B

Source and permissions

List recording length, format, audio quality, speaker permissions, slides, music, brand assets and any confidential sections. Confirm who may approve excerpts and which claims or quotations require extra checks.

C

Deliverables and revisions

Agree the types of assets, aspect ratios, caption files, editable-file requirements, languages, review rounds and delivery format. Clip counts depend on the quality and substance of the recording; more clips are not automatically better.

D

Publishing responsibilities

Name the person who signs off final copy, who schedules approved posts, and who answers comments or direct messages. Social scheduling and community management are separate responsibilities unless explicitly included in the scope.

A brief can be simple: one paragraph about the audience, a link to the source recording, examples of preferred styles, an approved call to action, a list of exclusions and one named reviewer. We can use that to discuss a realistic scope rather than quote a fixed package without seeing the material.

QUALITY AND ACCOUNTABILITY

Approval, handover and measurement

Before editing: agree access to source files, reuse rights, storage and deletion expectations, a contact for factual questions and the permitted use of AI-assisted transcription or editing tools. Sensitive or restricted footage should not be uploaded to third-party services without the appropriate permission.

Before publishing: check subtitles against the original audio, preserve context, confirm brand and speaker approval, test any links and record the final version. If an asset contains a statistic or a regulated claim, the responsible client reviewer should verify it against the source.

After publishing: review the measures that fit the goal. Useful indicators may include relevant website visits, qualified conversations, newsletter sign-ups, content saves and audience retention where those metrics are available. Views alone do not establish sales or lead quality. No specific outcome is guaranteed.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

Do you need a professional recording?

Not necessarily, but source audio and video quality affect the final result.

How many clips will I receive?

The number, lengths and revisions are agreed in the project scope.

Can you publish the content too?

Yes, scheduling can be combined with social media management when agreed.

Who approves the clips and captions?

We agree one or more responsible reviewers before production. Final public-facing assets are submitted for client approval, and nothing is scheduled without the agreed sign-off.

Can you work with confidential recordings?

Only after the permitted use, access controls, editing tools and handling requirements have been agreed. Restricted material should be excluded or redacted where appropriate.
